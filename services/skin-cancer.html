Skin Cancer Screening

Early detection saves lives. Comprehensive skin cancer screening and expert treatment by board-certified dermatologists.

Protect Your Skin, Protect Your Life

Skin cancer is the most common cancer in the United States, but when detected early, it's highly treatable. At Smart Skin Dermatology, our board-certified dermatologists provide comprehensive skin cancer screenings to catch suspicious spots early and provide expert treatment when needed.

Types of Skin Cancer We Detect & Treat

  • Basal Cell Carcinoma (BCC): The most common skin cancer, typically appearing as a pearly or waxy bump
  • Squamous Cell Carcinoma (SCC): Often appears as a firm red nodule or scaly patch
  • Melanoma: The most dangerous form of skin cancer, arising from pigment-producing cells
  • Precancerous Lesions: Actinic keratoses and other precancerous growths

Know Your ABCDEs

Use this guide to evaluate moles and spots on your skin:

  • A - Asymmetry: One half doesn't match the other
  • B - Border: Irregular, ragged, or blurred edges
  • C - Color: Multiple colors or uneven distribution
  • D - Diameter: Larger than 6mm (size of a pencil eraser)
  • E - Evolution: Changing in size, shape, or color

What to Expect During a Skin Exam

A full-body skin exam is quick, painless, and thorough:

  • You'll change into a gown for the examination
  • Our dermatologist will systematically examine your entire skin
  • Suspicious spots may be photographed for monitoring
  • If needed, a biopsy can often be performed the same day
  • The exam typically takes 10-15 minutes

Who Should Get Screened?

We recommend annual skin cancer screenings for most adults, especially those with:

  • History of significant sun exposure or sunburns
  • Fair skin, light eyes, or red/blonde hair
  • Family or personal history of skin cancer
  • Multiple moles or atypical moles
  • History of tanning bed use

Frequently Asked Questions

How often should I get a skin cancer screening?

We recommend annual full-body skin exams for most adults. If you have a history of skin cancer, significant sun exposure, or multiple moles, more frequent screenings may be recommended.

Is skin cancer screening covered by insurance?

Most insurance plans cover skin cancer screenings, especially if you have risk factors. We recommend calling your insurance provider to verify coverage before your appointment.

What happens if something suspicious is found?

If we identify a suspicious spot, we can often perform a biopsy the same day. The tissue is sent to a lab for analysis, and results are typically available within 1-2 weeks.
