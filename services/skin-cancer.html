Skin Cancer Screening

Early detection saves lives. Comprehensive skin cancer screenings and expert treatment from board-certified dermatologists in Lehi, Utah.

Protecting Your Skin Health

Skin cancer is the most common cancer in the United States, but when detected early, it's also one of the most treatable. At Smart Skin Dermatology, our board-certified dermatologists are experts at identifying and treating skin cancer, giving you peace of mind about your skin health.

⚠️ Utah Has High UV Exposure

Living at high altitude in Utah means increased UV exposure year-round. Regular skin cancer screenings are especially important for Utah residents to catch any concerns early.

Know the ABCDEs of Melanoma

Use this guide to monitor moles and spots between your dermatology visits:

A
Asymmetry

One half doesn't match the other

B
Border

Edges are irregular or blurred

C
Color

Multiple colors or uneven shading

D
Diameter

Larger than 6mm (pencil eraser)

E
Evolution

Changes in size, shape, or color

Types of Skin Cancer We Treat

  • Basal Cell Carcinoma: The most common type, usually appearing as a pearly bump or flat lesion
  • Squamous Cell Carcinoma: Often appears as a firm, red nodule or flat lesion with a scaly surface
  • Melanoma: The most serious form, often appearing as a new dark spot or changes in an existing mole
  • Precancerous Lesions (Actinic Keratoses): Rough, scaly patches that can develop into cancer if untreated

What to Expect During Your Screening

A comprehensive skin cancer screening includes:

  • Complete head-to-toe skin examination
  • Dermoscopy (magnified examination) of concerning spots
  • Discussion of your personal risk factors
  • Recommendations for monitoring or treatment
  • Education on sun protection and self-examination

Treatment Options

If skin cancer is detected, we offer comprehensive treatment options including:

  • Surgical excision with careful attention to cosmetic outcomes
  • Cryotherapy (freezing) for precancerous lesions
  • Topical treatments for certain superficial cancers
  • Referral for Mohs surgery when indicated
  • Coordination with oncology for advanced cases

Frequently Asked Questions

How often should I get a skin cancer screening?

We recommend annual full-body skin exams for most adults. If you have a history of skin cancer, significant sun exposure, fair skin, or multiple moles, more frequent screenings may be recommended.

What are the warning signs of skin cancer?

Watch for the ABCDEs: Asymmetry, Border irregularity, Color variation, Diameter larger than 6mm, and Evolution (changes over time). Any new, changing, or unusual spot should be evaluated by a dermatologist.

Is a skin cancer screening covered by insurance?

Most insurance plans cover annual skin cancer screenings as preventive care. We recommend checking with your insurance provider to confirm your coverage.

How can I reduce my risk of skin cancer?

Practice sun safety: use broad-spectrum SPF 30+ sunscreen daily, wear protective clothing and hats, seek shade during peak sun hours (10am-4pm), and avoid tanning beds.
