Rashes & Skin Discoloration

Expert diagnosis and treatment for all types of skin changes, rashes, and discoloration conditions.

Comprehensive Care for Skin Changes

Rashes and skin discoloration can be caused by a wide variety of conditions, from allergic reactions to chronic skin diseases. At Smart Skin Dermatology, our board-certified dermatologists are experts in identifying the cause of your skin changes and providing effective treatment.

Types of Rashes We Treat

  • Contact Dermatitis: Allergic or irritant reactions from soaps, cosmetics, plants, or metals
  • Hives (Urticaria): Raised, itchy welts that appear suddenly
  • Rosacea: Facial redness, flushing, and sometimes small bumps
  • Seborrheic Dermatitis: Scaly, flaky patches often on the scalp and face
  • Drug Rashes: Reactions to medications
  • Heat Rash: Blocked sweat ducts causing small bumps
  • Viral Rashes: Rashes accompanying viral infections
  • Fungal Infections: Ringworm, athlete's foot, and yeast infections

Skin Discoloration Conditions

  • Melasma: Brown or gray-brown patches, often on the face
  • Vitiligo: Loss of skin pigment causing white patches
  • Post-Inflammatory Hyperpigmentation: Dark spots after acne or injury
  • Age Spots (Solar Lentigines): Sun-induced brown spots
  • Birthmarks: Including café-au-lait spots and port-wine stains
  • Tinea Versicolor: Fungal infection causing lighter or darker patches

Our Diagnostic Approach

Accurate diagnosis is crucial for effective treatment. Our evaluation may include:

  • Detailed medical history review
  • Visual examination with dermoscopy
  • Patch testing for allergies
  • Skin scraping or culture for infections
  • Skin biopsy when needed for definitive diagnosis

Treatment Options

Based on your diagnosis, treatment may include:

  • Topical Medications: Corticosteroids, antifungals, antibiotics, or retinoids
  • Oral Medications: Antihistamines, antibiotics, or immunosuppressants
  • Phototherapy: Light-based treatments for certain conditions
  • Laser Treatments: For pigmentation and vascular issues
  • Chemical Peels: For hyperpigmentation and melasma
  • Allergen Avoidance: Identifying and avoiding triggers

When to Seek Immediate Care

Contact us right away or seek emergency care if you experience:

  • Rash accompanied by fever or difficulty breathing
  • Rapidly spreading rash
  • Blisters or peeling skin
  • Signs of infection (pus, warmth, spreading redness)
  • Rash affecting eyes, mouth, or genitals

Frequently Asked Questions

How do I know if my rash is serious?

Seek immediate care if your rash is accompanied by fever, difficulty breathing, rapid spreading, or signs of infection. Most rashes aren't emergencies, but persistent or worsening symptoms warrant a dermatology evaluation.

Can melasma be cured?

While melasma can be challenging to treat, it can be significantly improved with consistent use of topical lightening agents, chemical peels, and strict sun protection. Maintenance therapy is often needed.

How long does it take for skin discoloration to fade?

This varies depending on the cause and depth of pigmentation. Superficial discoloration may improve in weeks to months, while deeper pigmentation may take 6-12 months or longer with treatment.
