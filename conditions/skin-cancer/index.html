Skin Cancer Screening & Treatment

Early detection is the key to successful skin cancer treatment. Trust our experienced dermatologists for comprehensive skin cancer care.

Comprehensive Skin Cancer Care

Skin cancer is the most common form of cancer in the United States, but it's also one of the most treatable when caught early. At Smart Skin Dermatology, our board-certified dermatologists have decades of experience in detecting and treating all types of skin cancer.

Types of Skin Cancer We Treat

  • Basal Cell Carcinoma (BCC): The most common type, typically appearing as a pearly or waxy bump, often on sun-exposed areas
  • Squamous Cell Carcinoma (SCC): Often appears as a firm, red nodule or flat lesion with a scaly surface
  • Melanoma: The most serious form, often appearing as a mole that changes in size, shape, or color
  • Actinic Keratoses: Pre-cancerous lesions that can develop into squamous cell carcinoma
  • Merkel Cell Carcinoma: A rare but aggressive form of skin cancer

The ABCDE Rule for Melanoma Detection

Watch for these warning signs in moles:

  • A - Asymmetry: One half doesn't match the other
  • B - Border: Irregular, ragged, or blurred edges
  • C - Color: Multiple colors or uneven distribution
  • D - Diameter: Larger than 6mm (pencil eraser size)
  • E - Evolving: Changes in size, shape, or color over time

Our Skin Cancer Screening Process

During your full-body skin exam, we will:

  • Systematically examine all areas of your skin
  • Use dermoscopy to examine suspicious lesions in detail
  • Document and monitor any concerning spots
  • Perform biopsies of suspicious lesions as needed
  • Discuss your individual risk factors and prevention strategies

Skin Cancer Treatment Options

Treatment depends on the type, size, location, and depth of the cancer:

  • Surgical Excision: Removing the cancer along with a margin of healthy tissue
  • Mohs Surgery: Precise layer-by-layer removal for complex or high-risk cancers (referred to specialists)
  • Curettage & Electrodesiccation: Scraping and burning for superficial cancers
  • Cryotherapy: Freezing pre-cancerous and some early cancerous lesions
  • Topical Treatments: Prescription creams for precancers and superficial cancers
  • Photodynamic Therapy: Light-activated treatment for certain skin cancers

Who Should Get Screened?

We recommend annual skin cancer screenings for:

  • Anyone with a history of skin cancer
  • People with a family history of melanoma
  • Those with many moles or atypical moles
  • Fair-skinned individuals
  • People with a history of sunburns or tanning bed use
  • Those with weakened immune systems
  • Anyone noticing changes in existing moles or new skin growths

Frequently Asked Questions

How often should I get a skin cancer screening?

We recommend annual screenings for most adults, especially those with risk factors. High-risk individuals may need more frequent exams.

Does a biopsy hurt?

We use local anesthesia to numb the area, so you shouldn't feel pain during the procedure. You may experience mild soreness afterward.

What happens if skin cancer is found?

We'll discuss the type and stage of the cancer and recommend the most appropriate treatment. Most skin cancers can be successfully treated in our office.

How can I prevent skin cancer?

Use broad-spectrum SPF 30+ sunscreen daily, wear protective clothing, avoid peak sun hours (10am-4pm), and never use tanning beds. Regular skin self-exams are also important.
